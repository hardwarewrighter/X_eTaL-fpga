X_eTaL-fpga

X_eTaL programs as hardware: a typed array program becomes a circuit, simulated and checked bit for bit against the X_eTaL evaluator, and on its way to a Tang Nano.

c := a + b on eight 16-bit numbers is eight adders; '+ r_/ v is a tree of adders; a step function over a state vector is registers and the logic between them. Every example here is an X_eTaL program first. Its output is the reference, and the intermediate form, the simulated circuit and the generated Verilog must all reproduce it. The first machine, HEX1000, is a 4-bit calculator in the spirit of the TI TMS1000, sized for a Tang Nano 1K; larger machines go to the Tang Nano 9K and 20K and the Tang Primer boards.

HEX1000

A 4-bit machine of our own design in the spirit of the TMS1000: a 4-bit accumulator, 64 words of 4-bit RAM, a ROM of 1024 instructions, a program counter that steps as a shift register (docs/machine.md). It is written in X_eTaL (the Hex1000 library: one instruction is a pure step function of the machine's state), and its ROM holds a program in its own instructions (rom/calc.asm, about 330 of them) that makes it an eight-digit hexadecimal calculator. Keys go in through its key matrix and the digits come out on its output latches, nothing else computing; every screen is checked against the specification, the Radix library. 12+34= takes the machine 1752 instructions, FFFFFFFF*FFFFFFFF= 16406: on a Tang Nano at 13.5 million instructions a second, about a millisecond. Try HEX1000 in your browser: the same program, X_eTaL running in the page. Or at a terminal: clone the repository and run just calc (keys 0-9 A-F + - * = X, q to quit); just demo-lib Hex1000 listing shows its ROM, page by page. It runs as a circuit on a Tang Nano 9K; next, a physical calculator on a Tang Nano 1K.

The physical calculator, in 3D first

HEX1000 is becoming a battery-powered calculator on a Tang Nano 1K: a 4x4 hexadecimal pad, four operator keys and an 8-digit 7-segment display. Before anything is soldered, the panel is modeled in X_eTaL (the Panel library: the font, the keys and display as arrays, where a click lands) and drawn two ways: interactively in a window (just mockup: click or type the keys) and rendered by Blender, below. Each key goes through the machine's own key scan running the calculator ROM; the render refuses to start unless the display ends on the evaluator's answer, the one the Tang Nano 9K gives.

Libraries

Each links to its documentation, generated by xetal doc from the ## comments in its source; every ## >> example there is run as a test. The documentation index also has the demo programs, a search, the built-ins, and each source file.

LibraryAliasWhat
Clockedck:clocked circuits as X_eTaL step functions: counters, accumulators, shift registers, a sequence detector, a RAM, and the harness that clocks them
Datapathdp:the vector examples at a stated width: the whole-array operations a circuit realizes directly
Fixedfx:fixed-width two's complement numbers at a stated width, the arithmetic a w-bit circuit does
Hex1000hx:HEX1000, a 4-bit machine in the spirit of the TMS1000: its state, one instruction as a pure step, a run harness, an assembler
Panelpn:HEX1000's calculator panel as arrays: the 7-segment font, the keys and display in 3D, where a click lands, a key pressed at the machine
Radixrx:an eight-digit calculator in any radix (hexadecimal, octal, decimal): the specification HEX1000's ROM program must meet
Uartua:a serial port (UART: 8 data bits, no parity, 1 stop bit) as X_eTaL step functions: a transmitter, a receiver, and the two looped back
